Income Tax Guide for India

Understanding income tax can help you make more informed financial decisions. This guide introduces the difference between India's old and new tax regimes and explains what to check when comparing them.

Tax laws can change. Always confirm the rules that apply to your specific financial year before relying on a calculation.

1. What Are the Old and New Tax Regimes?

India's income tax system provides different tax-regime options for eligible taxpayers. The regimes can differ in their tax rates, deductions, exemptions, and eligibility conditions.

The old regime may allow certain deductions and exemptions when the taxpayer meets the relevant conditions. The new regime generally uses a different structure and has different rules for deductions and exemptions.

The regime that results in a lower tax amount depends on your income, eligible claims, and the rules applicable to you. Neither regime is automatically best for everyone.

2. Understand Your Income

Start by identifying your income for the relevant financial year. Depending on your circumstances, income may come from salary, business or professional activity, house property, capital gains, interest, or other sources.

Different types of income may be subject to different calculation and reporting rules. A simple calculator may not handle every income category or special tax treatment.

3. Review Eligible Deductions and Exemptions

Some taxpayers may qualify for deductions or exemptions, depending on the tax regime selected and the applicable conditions. Eligibility, documentation, limits, and rules can vary.

Do not assume that a deduction available under one regime is also available under the other. Verify each item against official guidance for the relevant financial year.

4. Consider Rebates, Cess, and Surcharge

The final tax amount may depend on more than the basic tax calculation. Rebates, health and education cess, surcharge where applicable, and other provisions may affect the result.

Check whether the calculator covers the relevant provisions and whether any eligibility conditions apply to your situation.

5. How to Compare the Regimes

  1. Identify the financial year for which you are calculating tax.
  2. Gather your income details for that year.
  3. Identify deductions and exemptions you may legally claim.
  4. Check which claims are allowed under each regime.
  5. Compare estimated tax amounts using the same income assumptions.
  6. Verify the results against current official rules before deciding.
